      In addition, we will read the entire work in English. Pick up an edition as soon as you can. Any modern translation will do. Please pace yourself to finish the Aeneid by the end of the semester.
      This course has been designed as an Independent Study. We will meet every two weeks. You will have prepared the assigned text well enough to read it with minimal difficulty. Since for most of you this is your first introduction to Latin prosody, I expect that the work required on your part will be considerable. The rewards will be commensurate.
      The links above will take you to the Latin text. The entire Latin text may be found at The Latin Library. For ease of translating and grammar review, I highly recommend Barbara Weiden Boyd's Vergil's Aeneid (2004). Also useful (but only for the first six books) is Pharr's Aeneid. Older, but still very useful for literary commentary and grammar, is Knapp's Aeneid. You may want to check the used bookstores on line. My favorite is Advanced Book Exchange.
      Do not hestitate to make use of your modern edition in order to understand the grammar of the Latin. Not all translations are grammatically faithful to the original. Here are some that may be of use:
           Aeneid (Williams) - English
           Aeneid (Dryden) - English. Also here.
           L'Énéide (Boxus et Poucet) - French
           Eneide (Caro) - Italian
      We will be reading approximately 180 lines per session (or about 90 lines per week). Please stay up with the work and come to the sessions with the assigned text prepared.
      Course Expectations:
           1. To read with reasonable facility the passages assigned.
           2. To recognize the basic rhetorical devices used by Virgil.
           3. To scan dactylic hexameter (we will do exercises).
           4. To articulate the differences between the "German School" and "Harvard School" in Vergilian studies and to defend a position of your own on the key issues involved.
      We will discuss this topic at length during our readings. For an overview of the issue read Schmidt, Ernst A. "The Meaning of Vergil's Aeneid: American and German Approaches" CW 94.2 (2001) 145-171. Download it here.
